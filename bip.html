BiP · Turkcell

Turkcell’s messaging super app: chats, calls, payments, crypto and services in one place.

RoleProduct Designer
Timeline2013 – 2015
PlatformiOS · Android
CompanyTurkcell

Project summary & challenges

BiP is Turkcell’s messaging app. It launched in 2013 and has passed 100 million downloads. Over time it grew from chats and calls into a super app with a wallet, a crypto exchange, third-party services and games. All of this was built by a large team of designers, product managers and engineers at Turkcell; this page shows the parts I worked on with them.

The challenge

  • Add payments, crypto, services and games without making messaging, the reason people open the app, feel heavier.
  • Serve very different users with one flow: Turkcell and other operators, new and existing Paycell users, Wi-Fi and mobile data.
  • Keep features built by different teams looking like one app.

My role

  • Designed screens and flows for onboarding, payments, messaging, status and the widget kit, including empty, loading and error states
  • Worked on the team that created BiP’s atomic design system, and drew icons for it
  • Made illustrations, in-app visuals and marketing material

A team effort: the screens on this page come from several BiP releases. I designed some of them end to end and contributed to others as part of the BiP design team.

#01

Onboarding

Four screens, then you’re in

Paycell inside BiP had to work for Turkcell and other operators, for new and existing Paycell users, on Wi-Fi and on mobile data. The flow became one path that branches only where it has to: a short intro, the phone number, then the check that fits each user.

#02

Payments

A wallet inside a messenger

Paycell lives in BiP’s More menu, next to calls, contacts and settings, so it’s one tap away without crowding the chat tabs.

The Paycell home leads with the balance and the three most common actions: top up, pay by QR and send money. Tapping the balance opens an accordion that splits it into card balance and credit limit, so the detail is there when people want it and out of the way when they don’t. Bills, gift cards and offers sit below.

#03

Digital assets

Crypto that feels like the rest of BiP

Digital Assets lets BiP users buy, sell and hold crypto, with Colendi running the exchange behind it. Most people opening it had never bought crypto, so the screens borrow what they already knew from Paycell: the same balance card, the same four round actions (buy, sell, add money, withdraw) and the same bottom sheets.

Four intro screens with 3D icons explain the product before sign-up. Buying is three steps in a bottom sheet over the wallet: pick an asset, enter an amount or tap 25 / 50 / 75 / 100% of the balance, check the summary. Identity checks, withdrawals and a searchable FAQ follow the same pattern.

#04

Widgets

A kit of widgets for every team

Services inside BiP were built by many teams, so we kept a shared kit of widgets on top of the design system: service rows with ratings and follow buttons, file cards, a weather card, campaign banners, a category grid for Discover, alerts for every state, a calendar, navigation and breadcrumbs, member lists, forms and buttons.

Every widget comes in BiP’s blue with the same radius, spacing and type, so a new service could be put together from parts that already matched the rest of the app.

#05

Messaging & Status

The reason people open the app

Chats stay clean: bubbles, quoted replies and read receipts, with calls one tap away in the header. Status lets people share a photo, video or text for 24 hours, add text, stickers and drawings, see who viewed it, and reply so the answer lands in the chat.

#06

Discover

Services inside BiP

Discover brings news and third-party services into the app. New users pick at least three interests, the home fills with matching services, and every service has its own page to follow, rate and review.

Pick, browse, follow

#07

Developer console

BiP for businesses, on the web

Companies and developers use BiP’s web console to reach customers through the app. Sign-up starts with one choice, individual or corporate, then a single form that asks only what that account needs: country, contact details and what they plan to use BiP for. A six-digit code confirms the account. The developer site sits next to it with documentation for payments, messaging and business apps.

Developer site

The documentation home groups the APIs by what a business wants to do: take payments in an app, talk to customers, manage messages and build business apps, each with a short card and its own page.

#08

Design system

An atomic design system, one icon language

As BiP grew, the team created an atomic design system: shared atoms such as colours, type and icons, built up into components and full screens, so every team could ship features that look like one app. I worked on it and drew icons for it. The icon set uses one stroke weight, rounded ends and the same corner radius across messaging, calls, media editing and payments.

Two illustration styles, one blue

Paycell’s intro uses a pixel style built from dots, matching the payment brand. Discover uses light line drawings with blue fills for its onboarding. Both stay in BiP’s blue so they sit naturally in the same app.

Looking back

What still holds up, and what I’d change

Some of these screens show their age: the type is small and the cards are busy. The structure still holds up: payments one tap from the chats, crypto built on the same patterns as the wallet, and a shared widget kit that kept many teams’ work looking like one app. Today I’d test flows with real users before polishing the screens, and I’d give the home fewer things to say.
