CitizenChain

A mobile app that turns everyday sustainability and civic action into levels, badges and a community.

RoleProduct Designer
Timeline2024 – 2025
PlatformMobile app
EngagementFreelance · MVP

Project summary & challenges

CitizenChain helps people take small, real actions for the planet and their community, such as cutting carbon, choosing safe food and water or sustainable fashion, and rewards them with levels, badges and a feed to share their progress. I designed the MVP for a client, from personas and flows to the final UI and motion. The app has not launched yet.

The challenge

  • Climate action feels too big for one person. The app had to break it into small tasks people can actually finish.
  • Motivation fades fast. Progress needed to be visible and rewarding, without feeling like a toy.
  • Six impact categories had to stay easy to browse on a phone screen.

My role

  • Personas and the MVP user flow
  • Two rounds of UI: the first SustainChain version and the final CitizenChain design
  • Onboarding, feed, category wheel, levels, rewards, profile and community screens
  • Category badges, animations and the prototype video

#01

Research

Three personas, one shared goal

The personas cover very different people who all want to do more for the planet: an eco-conscious millennial who wants a community, a new parent who wants a healthier world for their child and has little time, and a corporate sustainability manager who has to move a whole company. Their common pain was the same: good intentions, no easy way to act and see the result.

#02

Iteration

From SustainChain to CitizenChain

The first round, still called SustainChain, was dark teal and looked like a social network: a feed of posts with likes and comments. The final CitizenChain design moved to a bold purple with neon category icons, and put progress at the centre: every home screen opens with “My Progress” and the category wheel, and the feed supports it instead of leading.

#03

Onboarding

Sign up, verify, then a three-step tour

“Be a digital activist” sets the tone. Sign-up offers email or Facebook, Apple and Google, with errors shown right under each field, and a four-digit code confirms the account. A short tour then explains the three ideas the app is built on: your impact journey, sharing with your community, and tracking progress with the wheel.

#04

Feed & wheel

Browse impact by category

Home opens with “My Progress”: one ring per category showing how far each has come. Below, a feed of climate stories from people you follow, with points, reactions and a share sheet for every social network.

The wheel at the bottom of every screen is the main navigation: spin it to filter the whole app by one category, such as Carbon Reduction or Safe Food & Water, or tap “All” to see everything.

Feed, reactions, sharing, supports

#05

Levels & rewards

Tasks, ranks and a treasure chest

Each category has its own level and a short task list: support actions, follow accounts, share posts, invite a brand or a friend. Finishing tasks fills the bar and moves you up four ranks, from Activist and Leader to Future Ambassador and Change Maker. Every new rank opens a treasure chest, followed by a short climate fact worth sharing, which earns more points.

Four ranks

Tasks and rewards

#06

Community

Profile, follows, invites, alerts

The profile shows who you are, what you supported last and your progress rings, so it doubles as a public scorecard. People can follow each other, invite friends with a link or through any messenger, and get notified when something happens.

Looking back

What worked, and what I’d test next

Moving progress to the top of the home screen turned the app from a feed into a game with a purpose, and the wheel gave six categories one simple control. Before launch I’d test the wheel with first-time users, since a spinning menu is fun but new, and check that the ranks feel earned rather than handed out.
