Dentistry.one

Teledentistry platform: video consultations with dentists, messaging, care coordination and patient forms.

RoleSenior UI/UX Designer
Timeline2019 – 2020
PlatformWeb · Mobile
CompanyMouthWatch

Project summary & challenges

Dentistry.one connects patients with a nationwide network of dentists through live or scheduled video visits, with care advisors who coordinate in-office visits and benefits, and oral health coaching between appointments. Its sister product Teledent gives dental practices their own video call room for remote exams.

The challenge

  • Many people put off the dentist because of time, travel and cost. A first consult had to be possible from home, in minutes.
  • Patients worried about remote diagnosis and the privacy of their health data. The product had to earn trust on every screen.
  • Two audiences, patients and providers, with very different jobs, on desktop, tablet and phone.

My role

  • Sole designer on Teledentistry and Dentistry.one, working with product owners and developers
  • Research: competitive analysis, data from the sister product, user feedback, SWOT and user interviews
  • User flows, 150+ wireframes and 100+ screens for video calls, messaging and billing
  • The design system and component library, the marketing website and animated prototypes

#01

Research

Nine steps, four audiences

The work ran in three stages: research (competitive analysis, data from the sister product Teledent, user feedback), plan (user stories, flows, the red routes that must never break) and explore (sketches, wireframes, prototypes). It also showed that Dentistry.one serves four audiences, each with a different reason to care: health plans, employers, dental service organisations and individuals and families.

#02

Flows & wireframes

150+ wireframes before any pixels

The patient flow runs from login or sign-up through the dashboard to choosing a dentist, filling in a form and picking an insurance option. Every screen on it was wireframed first, more than 150 in total, and tested as a clickable prototype before the visual design started.

#03

Interviews

What patients liked, and feared

Video interviews with users confirmed the idea and showed where the design had to work harder.

What worked
  • Convenience was the main benefit: no travel time, no travel cost.
  • Accessibility improved for people in remote areas and with limited mobility.
  • Teleconsultations felt right for check-ups and minor issues, with quick access to advice.
What worried people
  • Diagnosis accuracy for complex problems, and the limits of a remote visit.
  • Privacy and data security of personal and health information.
  • Technical issues: dropped connections and glitches during calls.

These answers shaped the UI: clear statements of what a video consult can and can’t do, privacy notes where data is asked for, and a waiting room and connection states in the video call.

#04

Patient web app

Home, forms, profile, appointments

Home answers “how can we help you?” with three paths: a care concierge, a dentist and educational resources, each with a clear price (free, or a paid video consult and health report). Patient forms became friendly cards, the profile holds personal, insurance and payment details in one place, and appointments show their status at a glance.

#05

Mobile

From a text message to a dentist

Many patients arrive through a referral: a text from their health plan with a link. The mobile flow takes them from that message to sign-up, into a message thread with the care team, and on to a live video consult with a dentist.

#06

Teledent video calls

Remote exams for dental practices

Teledent is the provider side: a video room each practice can brand as its own. The host lets patients in from a waiting room, switches between gallery, spotlight and sidebar layouts, pins the camera that matters, and captures still images of the patient’s teeth during the call, which are saved straight to the case.

#07

Style guide

Icons and illustrations

A rounded line icon set in light and dark versions covers media, camera, calendar, messaging and calls, and friendly illustrations of patients and dentists keep a clinical product from feeling cold. Both sit in the component library the developers built from.

#08

Website

Dentistry without walls

The marketing site speaks to dentists who might join the network: what a virtual practice looks like, which appointments work over video, how the network is built and how patients find them. Scroll inside the frame to see the whole page.

Looking back

What worked, and what I’d do differently

Wireframing every screen first meant the product owners and developers agreed on the flows before any visual work, which saved rounds of rework. The interviews told us trust mattered as much as convenience; next time I’d bring those concerns into testing earlier, with a prototype of the video call itself.
