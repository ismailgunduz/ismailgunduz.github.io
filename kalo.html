Kalo

AI calorie tracker: take a photo of a meal and get calories, macros and a health score in seconds.

RoleSenior Product Designer
Timeline2025
PlatformiOS
CompanyOak Smart

Project summary & challenges

Kalo turns a photo of a meal into calories, carbs, protein and fat, adds a health score and small tips, and tracks progress toward a weight goal. It launched on the App Store in August 2025 and holds a 4.8★ rating.

The challenge

  • Calorie tracking is tedious. Logging a meal had to take seconds, not a search through a food database.
  • A personal plan needs height, weight, goals and habits, without onboarding that feels like a form.
  • Most people stop tracking after a few days. The app had to give them reasons to come back.

My role

  • Designed the onboarding and the personal plan
  • Designed meal scanning and the meal detail screen
  • Designed the daily home, profile and goal progress
  • Designed the achievement badges, premium banners, paywall versions and system pop-ups

Built together with Oak Smart’s product and engineering teams.

#01

Onboarding

A personal plan in a few taps

Two welcome screens say what Kalo does: snap a meal, get the numbers. Then one question per screen with big tappable answers, and rulers instead of text fields for height, weight and target weight. A thin progress bar at the top shows how much is left.

One question per screen

The answers turn into a plan

The last screen pays off the questions: a daily calorie target, a split for carbs, protein and fat, a chart of the expected weight curve and a short list of how to get there, with the sources the plan is based on.

#02

Snap a meal

From photo to macros in seconds

The camera opens straight into a frame for the plate, with a barcode mode one tap away. The meal page shows the photo, portion and calories first, then carbs, protein and fat as rings, a health score out of 10, and two small nudges: how long a walk burns it off, and a healthier alternative. Diet tags flag gluten, GMO, lactose and sugar.

Camera to meal detail

#03

Daily home

Today, at a glance

Home starts with a week strip and one big number: calories left for today. Under it, carbs, protein and fat, then the day’s meals from breakfast to snacks, a water tracker filled one glass at a time, and the day’s activities. The scan button sits in the tab bar, always one tap away.

#04

Progress & badges

Reasons to come back tomorrow

The goal page shows the weight curve from the first day to the target, with today marked on it, and the daily calorie and macro targets. Profile keeps it short: what’s been lost so far, how far to the goal, then settings.

Badges reward the small wins, like the first kilo lost or a streak, from bronze and silver up to gold, and appear on the goal page as they are earned.

Achievement badges

#05

Premium

Banners and three paywall versions

Premium shows up as friendly banners in the app, with the avocado and broccoli mascots, and as a paywall that leads with the promise: smart calorie tracking, reminders and nutrition insights. The paywall came in three versions: two with the free trial off and a different highlight on the short plan, and one with the trial on, where the button becomes “Try for $0” and the price line spells out what happens after the free week.

#06

States

When something goes wrong

System messages use the same mascot and one clear action: try again when the connection drops, okay when no purchase is found to restore, update when the app is out of date.

Looking back

What worked, and what I’d try next

Rulers and one-question screens made a long onboarding feel short, and the meal page answers the only question people have after a photo: how much was that? Next I’d bring the badges onto the home screen, where people look every day, instead of keeping them on the goal page.
